kicker: '03'
title: Site Energy
dek: Annual purchased energy by end use and variant.
---

"Site energy" is the energy the utility delivers to the house each year: heating, cooling, hot water, appliances,
lighting, and the fans and pumps that run the equipment. It is the figure behind the annual energy bill, and the
one used to judge a "net-zero" balance against on-site solar. Heating demand, used elsewhere in this report,
counts the heat the rooms need; the purchased heating energy is smaller because the heat pump delivers about two
units of heat for each unit of electricity in this climate.

All variants are modeled all-electric, with heat-pump heating, cooling and hot water, which is the design intent
for the project.

| Variant | Purchased energy |
| --- | ---: |
| Code Minimum | about 47,100 kWh/yr |
| **As-Drawn (Recommended)** | **about 33,800 kWh/yr** |
| EnerPHit By Component | about 25,000 kWh/yr |
| PHI Low Energy Building | about 20,400 kWh/yr |
| EnerPHit By Demand | about 18,300 kWh/yr |

Two things to note in the results: Heating dominates in the code-minimum case and falls fastest as the
envelope improves, which is why the envelope work returns more impact than anything else on this project. The
As-Drawn package uses about **28% less purchased energy than code minimum**, and its fan and pump energy is less
than half, because the tighter house and the ERV need less of both. Past that point, hot water and appliances set
the floor on annual consumption, and the remaining levers are equipment selection, occupant habits, and any future
rooftop solar.

The step from As-Drawn to EnerPHit By Component, about 8,800 kWh/yr, is the price of the uninsulated crawlspace
floor, the LePage windows, the walls without exterior insulation and the junction detailing together.

These are design estimates for comparing options, and they will differ from utility bills, which depend on the
weather in a given year and how the house is used.
