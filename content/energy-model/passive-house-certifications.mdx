#### Recommendation

Use the PHI EnerPHit "By Component" targets for every assembly that is being rebuilt, and use the standard
as the guide for the envelope, thermal bridging, airtightness and ventilation. Build the As-Drawn package well,
verify it with blower-door testing and ventilation commissioning, and skip the certification submission. The
retained fireplaces, the crawlspace floor that cannot be insulated, and the choice of LePage windows each
sit outside the EnerPHit component limits.

#### The two EnerPHit routes

_EnerPHit_ is PHI's retrofit standard, and it can be earned two ways: The **by-component method** sets a limit for each
assembly and window and ignores the whole-building demand figure. The **by-demand method** by contrast sets a whole-building heating-demand limit instead. Both routes must also meet the general criteria for airtightness (1.0 ACH50) and renewable primary energy.

The As-Drawn package is at or near the component targets at the roofs, the exterior-insulated walls, the insulated
cellar walls and floor, the airtightness limit and the ventilation heat recovery. The **EnerPHit By Component**
variant in this report shows the full component-method package: the same house with U-0.16 windows, an insulated
crawlspace floor, R-18 at the walls without exterior insulation, and EnerPHit-level junction detailing. The demand
route would take R-60 roofs, R-40 walls, R-30 cellar floors throughout and U-0.14 windows, and it still assumes
floor insulation this house cannot get. Those variants are in the report to price the targets, and we do not
recommend building either of them.
