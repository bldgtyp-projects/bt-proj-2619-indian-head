dek: The five renovation options compared in the energy model.

This assessment tests the building in five variants. Every variant assumes electric heat pumps for heating, cooling and hot
water, and every variant carries the same 0.40 SHGC glazing, so the differences below come from the envelope and
ventilation inputs alone. The **As-Drawn variant**  is the recommendation; the other four are here for comparison.

1. **Code Minimum:** A benchmark which assumes the rennovation work is built to meet the prescriptive minimums of the
   [2022 Connecticut State Building Code](/energy_model/#energy-code) for new construction:
   code-level assembly R-values, windows at about U-0.27, 3.0 ACH50 airtightness, and a typical "extract-only"
   (bath fan) ventilation system. It exists to give the other variants a common reference.

2. **As-Drawn (Recommended):** The renovation as shown in the current drawing set: the assembly studies in the
   [envelope](/building_envelope/) section, the owner's preferred LePage wood windows at an installed U-0.20, 1.0 ACH50 airtightness, a 20%
   thermal-bridge allowance, and a balanced ERV modeled at about 73% effective heat recovery. The fireplaces stay,
   and the crawlspace floor stays uninsulated at R-1.4. This is the package we recommend building in this case.

3. **EnerPHit By Component:** In this variant, As-Drawn package is brought to prescriptive **PHI EnerPHit By-Component** level: windows at  U-0.16, R-10 at the crawlspace floor, R-18 at the walls without exterior insulation, and a 15% thermal-bridge
   allowance on the strength of EnerPHit-level junction detailing. Heating demand falls to about 25,800 kWh/yr,
   about 15,500 kWh/yr below As-Drawn. Note that to fully reach this level, the crawlspace floors would need to be insulated, the north-side walls (property line) insulation improved, and the fireplaces sealed off.

4. **PHI Low Energy Building:** This vartiant incluces further envelope upgrades to meet the PHI "Low Energy Building" level of performance; U-0.16 windows, 0.8 ACH50, and 2 inches of insulation on the outdoor-air ducts. Heating demand lands at about **17,800 kWh/yr** against this building's limit of **18,400 kWh/yr** for that standard.

5. **EnerPHit By Demand:** The package required to meet the full performance-based **PHI-EnerPHit By-Demand** limit:
   R-60 roofs, R-40 above-grade walls and garage ceiling, R-35 cellar walls, R-30 cellar floors, windows at about
   U-0.14, 0.6 ACH50, and an ERV at about 74% effective heat recovery. Heating demand lands at about
   **15,200 kWh/yr** against a limit of **15,300 kWh/yr**. It assumes far more floor insulation than this house
   allows, so it is here for comparison only.

_R-values are effective whole-assembly values in hr-ft²-F/Btu. Window U-values are installed whole-window values in
Btu/hr-ft²-F, averaged across the house._
