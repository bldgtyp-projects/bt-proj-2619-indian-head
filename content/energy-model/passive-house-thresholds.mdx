[Total annual energy consumption](/energy_model/#site-energy) is the key figure when evaluating a building’s performance, as shown above. The ‘Passive House’ framework adds heating and cooling annual energy demand targets on top of that top line figure. The peak-heating and peak-cooling loads are worth comparing against the same limits. Certification does not require every one of these limits, but meeting them where possible is good practice.

The limits shown against each result below are the PHI EnerPHit energy-demand limits for this climate. They are the strictest reference points available for a retrofit of this type, and only the last variant meets the heating target. That is the expected outcome for a landmarked masonry row house with a large north-facing front elevation. It is also why our recommendation is calibrated to the peak loads and to the achievable envelope, and not to the demand limit.

The **peak heating and peak cooling loads** are the numbers the mechanical designer will want in order to properly evaluate the system capacity. Under the **As-Drawn variant** the whole-building peak heating load falls to roughly 17,500 W (60 kBtu/hr) and peak sensible cooling to roughly 5,000 W (17 kBtu/hr). Both are small enough for a standard compact all-electric system.
