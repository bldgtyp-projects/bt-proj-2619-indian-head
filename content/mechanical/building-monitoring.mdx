#### Environmental monitoring

Install temperature, humidity and CO<sub>2</sub> sensors in the cellar, a bedroom, the top-floor office and the
sunroom. The hardware is inexpensive, and it is how the team tunes the ventilation schedule and tracks down comfort
questions over the first year: humidity trends in the cellar, CO<sub>2</sub> in the bedrooms overnight, and the
afternoon temperature swing in the sunroom each point to a different adjustment.

[Netatmo](https://www.netatmo.com/smart-indoor-air-quality-monitor) is the reference for indoor temperature,
humidity and CO<sub>2</sub> monitoring. It uploads over the house wireless network and is readable online. Choose
the room coverage before ordering, and agree how the project team will see the trends at the seasonal follow-ups.

#### Circuit-level energy monitoring

A branch-circuit monitor is the only way to see where the house's energy goes. Monitor the heat pumps, the water
heater, the ERV and the makeup-air heater on their own circuits. Watching them over the first months catches
equipment running wrong: unexpected backup resistance heat, a fan that never slows down, a schedule that differs
from the design. It also lets the measured use be compared with the model after the weather and the household are
accounted for.

If the electrical panel is being replaced, a load center with integrated monitoring such as the
[Leviton Smart Load Center](https://leviton.com/products/residential/load-centers/the-leviton-smart-load-center)
is the cleanest option.

<figure class="btwr-section-hero"><img src="/assets/mechanical/monitor-leviton.jpg" alt="Smart load-center breakers, metering modules, and monitoring app." /></figure>

For a conventional panel, a separate monitor such as [eGauge](https://www.egauge.net/) bolts on, with a current
sensor on each monitored circuit. Coordinate the number of circuits, the sensor space in the panel and the
networking with the electrician.

<figure class="btwr-section-hero"><img src="/assets/mechanical/monitor-egauge.jpg" alt="Circuit-level energy dashboard showing load and usage history." /><figcaption>Example monitoring dashboard.</figcaption></figure>

Review the first heating and cooling seasons with the owner, using these trends to adjust the ventilation schedule
and investigate any comfort questions. The [Resilience](/resilience/) page names the rooms most worth watching.
