title: Ventilation System Passive House Requirements
dek: The ventilation details that Passive House practice requires, applied to this house.
---

Passive House ventilation practice sets the standard for this system whether or not the house is certified. The
following requirements apply to the design and to commissioning:

- Total measured supply and extract airflows are within 10% of each other.
- Every bedroom and living room receives fresh supply air; every bathroom, the laundry and the kitchen area have
  extract.
- Ventilation air comes directly from outdoors, never from the garage, the crawlspace or the attic.
- Outdoor air passes through a MERV 13 filter before distribution. The filter is changed at the end of
  construction, and the house is ventilated before occupancy.
- The filter is located where the owner can reach and change it.
- The outdoor-air intake sits at least 10 ft from the garage, the kitchen exhaust, the fireplace flues and other
  pollutant sources, and at least 5 ft from the ventilation exhaust outlet (10 ft recommended), with snow
  exposure and service access checked.
- Outdoor-air ducts inside the house are insulated with a continuous, sealed vapor barrier through every elbow,
  joint, wall sleeve and unit connection.
- Ducts and terminals are sized for quiet operation, the unit is isolated from the structure, and transfer paths
  stay open when doors are shut.
- Bathroom extract meets at least 20 cfm continuous or 50 cfm on boost. Kitchen extract through the ERV meets at
  least 25 cfm continuous, with the register at least 6 ft from the cooktop and a washable grease filter.
- Grease-laden cooking exhaust goes through the range hood, never through the ERV.
- Normal and boost controls are simple and reachable, and the owner receives a written maintenance schedule. The
  heating and cooling thermostat does not control the fresh air.
- Net pressure across the envelope with all equipment running stays within plus or minus 5 Pa.
