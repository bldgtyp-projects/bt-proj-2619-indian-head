#### Equipment selection

For this house we recommend a high-performance ERV with better than 80% rated heat recovery and MERV 13
filtration. The [Zehnder ComfoAir Q600](https://zehnderamerica.com/products/ventilator/comfoair-q/) is the
reference unit: at the 300 cfm design flow it has capacity to spare for the pressure drop through the ducts,
silencers, filters and terminals, and its filtration and sound levels suit a house of this quality. Have the
supplier confirm the Q600 or an equivalent against the completed room schedule, and require product data for heat
and moisture recovery, fan power, frost protection and sound at the operating point.

Locate the unit where the filters can be changed easily, with a condensate drain and electrical connection, and
as close to the exterior wall as possible. The model assumes about **16 ft of outdoor-air duct with 1 inch of
insulation**; shorter runs and 2 inches of insulation raise the effective heat recovery and are a low-cost
improvement. Every outdoor-air duct must stay continuously vapor-tight, since it runs cold through the house.

#### Test and balance

The values in this analysis are design assumptions. Include design support and field balancing in the ventilation
contractor's or supplier's scope, and complete commissioning before occupancy:

1. Install clean filters and the final terminals, and confirm the ducts and outdoor connections are complete.
2. Measure each room's supply or extract flow at the normal and boost settings, and adjust the terminals to the
   agreed schedule.
3. Bring the total supply and extract within **10%** of each other.
4. Check the transfer paths with the doors closed, the controls, the boost operation, the frost strategy, and the
   fan noise in the bedrooms.
5. Run the kitchen hood and its makeup-air system together and confirm they do not disturb the fireplaces.
6. Hand the owner the measured balancing report, the normal settings, the filter type and replacement schedule,
   and the service contacts.

Repeat the flow checks after any later change to terminals or ductwork.
