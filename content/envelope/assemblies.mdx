The assembly calculations below document the **As Drawn variant**, which is the package we
recommend building. The design team should review each assembly and confirm it suits the project; the drawings are performance targets and coordination references rather than construction details.

All R-values are **effective whole-assembly** values as modeled: They account for framing, fasteners and the other thermal bridges inside the assembly, so they run lower than the nominal R-value of the insulation alone, and they are the numbers that drive the energy results.

| Assembly | As-Drawn (Recommended) | EnerPHit By Component |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| New metal roof | **R-50.5** | - |
| Flat roof | **R-49.8** | - |
| Exterior wall with exterior insulation | **R-34.5** | - |
| Exterior wall without exterior insulation | **R-12.8** | R-18 |
| Cellar wall, interior insulation | **R-16.4** | - |
| Cellar wall, exterior insulation | **R-12.4** | - |
| Garage ceiling | **R-31.7** | - |
| Insulated cellar floor | **R-21.9** | - |
| Remaining crawlspace floor | **R-1.4, existing** | R-10 |
| Windows, installed | **U-0.20, LePage** | U-0.16 |
| Airtightness | **1.0 ACH50** | 1.0 ACH50 |
| Thermal-bridge allowance | **+20%** | +15% |

**Crawlspace floor:** The **As-Drawn variant** leaves this floor in its existing condition with no insulation. The EnerPHit By Component column shows what insulating it would be worth: about 5,200 kWh/yr of heating demand through the floor itself, plus its share of the thermal-bridge allowance. Raising the north-side walls without exterior insulation from R-12.8 to R-18 is worth a further 600 kWh/yr or so, and is in that column for completeness.

**Thermal Briding:** With the field of each assembly at these levels, the remaining heat loss concentrates at the connections: window returns, rim joists, the foundation transition, roof intersections, porch and canopy connections, and the sunroom tie-in. The **EnerPHit By-Component variant** assumes those junctions are detailed to the EnerPHit level, modeled as a 15% thermal-bridge allowance against the 20% carried in As-Drawn, and that assumption is worth about 10,000 kWh/yr of heating demand in the model, the largest single step between the two columns. It depends on buildable details at those junctions in the next phase, and it is the first place to look if the owner wants more than the **As-Drawn variant** delivers.
