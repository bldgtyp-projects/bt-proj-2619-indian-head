The summer study shows the exposed upper-story and dormer windows receiving far more sun than the windows sheltered by porches and overhangs. The sunroom includes both shaded and exposed glazed faces, so its summer comfort depends on how the exposed faces are handled.

The As-Drawn package reports about **2,100 kWh/yr of sensible cooling demand**, a small number for a house of this size. A low annual total describes the house as a whole; the exposed rooms and the sunroom can still run warm on a sunny afternoon, and the [Resilience](/resilience/) study shows the same rooms carrying the summer outage result. Solar control at those windows is where the effort belongs.

### Recommendations

- Develop exterior shading at the sunroom and the exposed upper glazing: overhangs, operable exterior shades, or   planting. Exterior devices stop the sun before it reaches the glass and outperform any interior product.
- Specify the interior shades as part of the window package. A light-colored, tightly woven blind blocks several   times the heat of a dark open-weave fabric, and the choice costs nothing in construction.
- Set the SHGC at the most exposed openings against both seasons: a lower value there cuts the summer gain directly, at a small cost in winter gain at those windows.
- Have the mechanical designer check the room cooling loads and dehumidification for the sunroom and the top floor separately from the whole-house figure.
